Understanding the problem. Studies of city traffic find about 30% of drivers in busy centres are just looking for parking (Shoup, 2006). INRIX measured 17 hours a year per driver. On our own campus there is no way to see a free bay before you drive in, so everyone circles.
02Why it keeps happening

First come,
first guessed.

The problem isn't too few bays. It's that nobody can see them, book them, or plan them.

01

No visibility

From the gate you can't tell which of 96 bays is empty, so you go and look.

02

No booking

Seats, trains and tables can all be booked. A 2.5 × 5 m bay still can't.

03

Blind operators

Nobody knows which rows fill first, or when, so nothing gets planned.

04

Wasted minutes

Every lap burns fuel and time, for you and for the queue behind you.

Four root causes: drivers can't see free bays, they can't book one, the people running the lot have no data, and all of that turns into circling, which wastes time and fuel.
03The idea

Book a bay the way you book a seat.

Ticket apps made picking your exact seat feel normal: you see the hall, you pick the empty one, it's held for you. Arrivo brings that to parking. You see every bay on a live plan, pick a free one, choose your hours, and it's yours when you arrive.

same idea, new place.
Originality. Everybody already understands seat maps from booking films. We asked: why not a seat map for parking bays? Watch the cinema turn into a parking lot. That one idea is the whole product.

11Architecture

How a booking travels.

Browser

Your phone

Live plan, lens, panel. Taps a free bay and books it.

Browser

A friend's laptop

Sees that bay turn booked the moment it happens.

server.py

Python server

Checks the rules, saves the booking, tells every open page. Also runs demo cars so the lot looks alive.

parkseat.db

SQLite

Bookings and the live feed. A trigger refuses any two bookings that overlap on one bay.

1tap a bay → 2POST the booking → 3save, or refuse a clash (409) → 4push to every screen

One booking, start to end. The phone sends the booking to our Python server. The server checks for a clash and saves it in SQLite in one locked step. Then it pushes the change to every open page over Server-Sent Events, so a friend's screen updates without refreshing.
12Under the hood

Four rules that keep it honest.

01 · no double booking

Two cars can never get one bay.

Checked inside a locked transaction, and again by a database trigger.

# two windows overlap when
if a.start < b.end and b.start < a.end:
    raise ApiError(409, "overlap")
02 · status from time

Every bay knows what it is.

Worked out from the clock and the day's bookings, never typed in by hand.

free      nothing booked now
booked    a car is in it
ending    due out in ≤ 20 min
reserved  due in within 30 min
yours     booked from this device
03 · live without refresh

Every screen updates at once.

One open connection per page. If it drops, the page reconnects and catches up.

Content-Type: text/event-stream

event: change
data: {"booking": {"bayId": "A-07", …}}
04 · private by default

No sign-up. No plates on show.

A random device ID marks your bookings, and only its hash is stored.

owner = sha256(device_id)

# other people's plates
"UP14 AB 4821"  →  "UP14 •• 4821"
Four engineering decisions. One: double booking is impossible, checked twice, in the code and in the database. Two: a bay's status is computed from time, so it's always right. Three: Server-Sent Events push changes to every screen. Four: no accounts, and other people's number plates are masked.

AAppendix

Questions we expect.

Q1What if someone books and never comes?

The bay waits 15 minutes past their arrival time, then opens up for anyone.

Q5Why not just use Google Maps?

Maps tells you a lot exists. Arrivo tells you which bay is free, and holds it for you.

Q2How do you know a bay is really empty?

Today, from bookings. Next, a QR check-in at the gate; later, cameras or sensors.

Q6How does it make money?

A small commission per booking, plus subscription plans for lot operators.

Q3Can two people book the same bay?

No. The server checks inside a locked step, and the database has a rule that refuses overlaps.

Q7Do I need to install an app?

No. It's a website that works in any phone browser. The server is one Python file.

Q4What about privacy?

No sign-up. A random device ID, stored only as a hash, and other people's plates are masked.

Q8What did you find hardest?

Making the plan match the real lot, and keeping every screen in sync without refreshing.
